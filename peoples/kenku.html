The Kenku

Notes on a people who travel lightly, remember everything, and somehow never run out of things to collect.

I · First impressions

Meet the wandering collectors

One seldom notices a Kenku Flight arriving all at once. First comes a familiar whistle, then a pair of dark wings overhead, and finally a procession of travellers carrying an astonishing number of little things.

Kenku are intelligent, feathered people with a fondness for conversation, unexpected discoveries and the sort of objects most travellers would leave behind. Many live in small travelling communities called Flights. Others settle down, at least until the road calls again.

They are not all alike. A coastal Flight may know every harbour bell by sound; a mountain Flight might keep a collection of stones that looks, to an outsider, suspiciously like ordinary gravel.

II · Life on the move

A home with no fixed address

Most Flights travel together by choice, sharing meals, routes, stories and responsibilities. A Flight may include relatives, old friends and newcomers who simply found that their journeys were better together.

There is no permanent captain in every Flight. The best navigator leads through difficult country; a skilled negotiator takes over at the market. Someone else, usually with considerable patience, keeps track of the collection.

Scouts travel ahead, while others move the shared belongings. Stops are frequent: a curious sound, an unfamiliar plant or an abandoned sign can interrupt even the most carefully planned journey.

Camp is where the day's finds are cleaned, compared and discussed. Some objects are traded away; others earn a place in the Flight's travelling collection.

A Gathering is part meeting, part reunion and part wonderfully noisy storytelling evening. Routes are discussed, news exchanged, disagreements settled and new finds shown around.

A useful distinctionA Flight is a travelling community, not necessarily a family or a permanent political group. Members may leave, join or begin a new Flight.

A Flight's route may be seasonal, spontaneous or carefully planned. It is a mistake to assume that every community follows the same pattern.

III · Collected curiosities

Nothing is quite worthless

Every Flight has its collection. It belongs to the community, not to whoever happened to find the objects first. A Keeper may catalogue and care for it, though everyone has a story to contribute.

Here are three ordinary-looking objects from an imagined travelling collection. Select one to read its catalogue label.

Collection no. 014A compass that points nowhere

The needle broke years ago. The Flight keeps it because it was carried across the mountains on their first journey together.

Collection no. 027A wooden spoon, carefully repaired

Not rare, not magical, and not for sale. It belonged to the cook who taught three generations how to prepare supper on the road.

Collection no. 031A tiny brass bell

Found beside an abandoned road. Nobody knows its owner. Its sound, however, is now used to announce the evening meal.

What does a Keeper actually do?

Keepers record where an object came from, who contributed it, what happened to it and why it matters. They repair what they can and make sure the collection survives the journey.

Do not confuse curiosity with theftKenku trade, receive gifts and own things like anyone else. The belief that they are all thieves is an old and rather tiresome stereotype.
“An object's story may be worth more than the object itself.”
IV · Voices and promises

Things worth remembering

A Kenku can have a voice entirely their own and still reproduce a friend's laugh, the creak of a door or a rainstorm on a tiled roof. In some communities, storytellers use these sounds to bring a journey back to life.

Promises are also taken seriously in many Flights. An important agreement may be marked with a small promise token: a bead, a feather, a key or any object chosen by the people involved.

When the promise is fulfilled, the token may be returned, exchanged or placed in the communal collection. The custom is widespread enough to be recognised, but its precise form differs from one Flight to another.

Listen closelyImitation is a talent, not a substitute for identity. A Kenku speaking in a borrowed voice is still speaking for themself.
Archivist's question ↗

What happens when a token is lost before a promise is fulfilled? Different communities may have very different answers. Further fieldwork required.

V · At the Grand Library

A place for wandering stories

Travelling Flights can bring news of forgotten roads, vanished settlements and objects whose histories were never written down. The Library welcomes such knowledge, but a community's collection is not automatically the Library's to acquire.

Some Kenku visit to study, some to exchange records, and some simply because a building containing this many books is rather difficult to ignore.
