The Kenku

Keepers of echoes, collectors of wonders.

A people of feather, memory and curiosity. Their voices carry the sounds of the world; their collections preserve the things others overlook.

01 · General classification

A familiar silhouette, an extraordinary mind.

Kenku are intelligent, avian humanoids known for their curiosity, sociability and remarkable ability to imitate sound. They form close communities, develop varied personal interests and maintain a special fondness for knowledge and music.

02 · Form & physiology

Between two forms

In their usual form, kenku possess an anthropomorphic avian anatomy: feathers, beaks, wings and other features reminiscent of crows and ravens. Their wings are functional, allowing flight.

They can also transform into ordinary crows, adopting the appearance and scale of the familiar bird.

Their plumage is predominantly dark. Depending on the angle and quality of light, their feathers may reveal subtle iridescent blues, purples and greens. Individual differences are more apparent in the shape of the beak, the eyes and other small anatomical details.

Their natural lifespan remains undocumented. Exceptionally long-lived individuals are known, although millennia of life are not considered typical of the species.

03 · Language & sound

A voice of their own

Kenku possess their own voices, generally low and resonant, and communicate through a native language built around sounds. Its structure and vocabulary remain subjects of ongoing study.

They are also natural mimics. A kenku can reproduce voices, animal calls and environmental sounds with extraordinary precision. This ability is an innate trait, not a sign that the speaker lacks an identity or an original voice.

Research status: The phonetics, grammar and regional variations of the kenku language have yet to be fully documented by the Library.
04 · Communities & customs

Many voices, many ways of life

Kenku commonly live in small communities and value the company of their own kind. Mutual respect and social bonds are important, but no single custom defines every community.

Curiosity is widespread, as is a love of music and learning. Many kenku collect small objects, trinkets and curiosities. The meaning of a collection is personal: one individual might treasure unusual stones, another discarded keys, old instruments, fragments of writing or curious sounds.

They are often shy when first meeting strangers, yet many become warm and sociable once trust has been established. Kenku generally interact well with other peoples, although attitudes towards outsiders vary between communities.

Kindness should not be mistaken for forgetfulness. A serious betrayal or act of cruelty may be remembered for a very long time, and some kenku will retaliate when threatened or harmed.

05 · Professions & pursuits

Knowledge takes many forms

Kenku can be found among librarians, musicians, explorers, researchers, artisans and travellers. Their curiosity and ability to reproduce sounds can be useful in many pursuits.

Some individuals also turn to theft, but this is an individual choice rather than an inherent feature of kenku identity.

There is no universal kenku profession, and communities differ in their traditions, skills and relationships with the wider world.
