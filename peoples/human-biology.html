Biological overview

One people, no single pattern.

Humans possess no universal extraordinary biological advantage. Their diversity lies in individual variation, inherited traits, environmental influences and the different ways people learn to work with—or live without—magic.

01 · General anatomy

Familiar bodies, countless variations

In their underlying anatomy, Humans of the realms closely resemble ordinary humans: their proportions, physical capabilities, reproduction and maturation follow familiar patterns. Strength, speed and endurance do not ordinarily exceed human limits, though magic can alter performance for a time.

Physical diversity is extensive, but no single appearance or trait represents humanity as a whole.

02 · Physical diversity

The inheritance of appearance

Natural hair colours and most features fall within the familiar human range. Violet, red or gold eyes may arise through magical environmental influence and can be passed from parent to child. Such traits are distinct from a spell that temporarily changes someone's appearance.

No extraordinary eye colour, unusual hair or magical aptitude determines a person's culture or character.

03 · Environmental adaptation

What the snow leaves behind

In some snowy regions, naturally white hair is especially common, even among the young. This characteristic reflects both inheritance and long exposure across generations to the magic of the environment; it is not universal among inhabitants of the cold.

When families leave these regions, white hair may become less common in later generations, depending on their ancestry. Environmental magical traits differ in how strongly they persist. Regional variation also includes modest differences in pigmentation and certain senses, rather than universal supernatural resistance to extreme temperatures.

04 · Growth and reproduction

A familiar course of life

Human pregnancy, birth, childhood and physical maturation generally follow the patterns familiar to human physicians. Individual development varies, but there is no separate universal timetable of growth for the Humans of the realms.

Whether Humans can have biological children with other sapient peoples remains a question to be considered separately for each pairing; no universal rule has been established.

05 · Ageing and longevity

Years gained, years borrowed

Most Humans live approximately seventy to ninety years. Their bodies age in recognisably human ways, although magical influences may temporarily change how ageing appears or progresses.

Alchemy, potions, rituals, spells and enchanted artefacts can sometimes prolong life. These are exceptional interventions, not a normal feature of human biology, and their safety and reliability vary.

06 · Magical affinity

The gift and the study

Magical affinity is chiefly hereditary. It may appear spontaneously in childhood, surface under intense emotion, be identified through testing, or go unnoticed until adulthood. An affinity does not itself confer skill.

Anyone may study magical theory, but those without affinity cannot cast spells directly, no matter how thoroughly they understand the instructions: an unaided attempt simply fails. Suitable magical artefacts can nevertheless allow them to channel magical effects. Manifestations of affinity vary by discipline and can include sensations, phenomena or temporary changes in appearance.

07 · Magical physiology

The limits of an inner reserve

Spellcasting draws upon an internal magical reserve, whose initial capacity varies from person to person. It replenishes through rest and may grow with practice and experience. External magical sources can supplement it.

The cost of magic also depends on the discipline and the practitioner's efficiency. Casting may cause physical fatigue; exceeding one's capacity can produce injury. Ordinary spell-induced changes to the body end when their magic ends, unlike inherited traits shaped by a magical environment.

08 · Mastery and overexertion

The discipline of control

Beginners may experience exhaustion, dizziness, fainting, unstable spells or physical injury after overuse. A trained practitioner learns to direct energy more precisely, conserve effort and withstand greater demands.

Complete mastery of a magical discipline is exceptionally rare. Such a master can perform powerful and intricate work with little effort and without exhaustion under normal conditions. This does not guarantee safety when attempting unfamiliar disciplines: an exceptional overload remains possible. Recovery from magical exhaustion may require food, sleep, prolonged rest, alchemical treatment or assistance from healers.

09 · Health and disease

Illnesses of flesh and magic

Humans experience ordinary diseases as well as illnesses of magical origin. Some magical conditions interfere with spellcasting, temporarily alter bodily functions, cause unusual symptoms or spread between individuals. Certain illnesses occur across several sapient peoples; others are associated with particular regions.

Human immune function broadly resembles ordinary human physiology, but magic can strengthen or impair it. Natural senses are familiar, with modest regional variations; spells can temporarily sharpen perception.

10 · Medicine and healing arts

Knowledge before the cure

Conventional medicine is the foundation of healthcare, particularly where magical practitioners are scarce. Hospitals may bring together physicians, alchemists and magical healers. Their disciplines complement rather than replace one another.

Healing magic demands extensive study, anatomical knowledge and practical expertise. A magical affinity alone does not make someone a healer. Treatment depends on the practitioner's skill and the energy required; serious injuries may be difficult to treat, and not every disease can be cured by magic. Physicians may also specialise in alchemy.
