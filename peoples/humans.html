Humans

The Many Paths of Humanity

One people, countless histories. Familiar in form, endlessly varied in culture, ambition and the ways they make their place in the realms.

01 · General classification

Humanity has no single story.

Humans are among the most widespread and varied peoples of the realms. They differ in appearance, customs, beliefs, languages and ways of life, shaped by the places they inhabit and the histories they inherit.

ClassificationHumanoid
LifespanTypically less than a century
AdulthoodGenerally reached in the late teens
OriginsMany kingdoms, cultures and realms
“Humanity is not a single story. It is a collection of millions of different ones.”

— An inscription in the Grand Library

There is no single human culture, nor any one trait that defines humanity. Humans can be cautious or reckless, scholarly or practical, generous or selfish, traditional or eager for change. Their adaptability allows them to make homes in isolated villages, wandering settlements, vast cities and distant planes.

02 · Culture & community

Many lives, many ways of belonging

Human communities differ greatly in traditions, social structures, clothing, architecture and relationships with magic. Some preserve customs passed down through generations; others embrace new ideas and reinvent themselves.

Humans are physically diverse, with a wide range of skin tones, facial features, body types and hair textures. Their names and languages reflect their varied origins and cultural backgrounds. No culture, appearance or tradition can represent all humans.

03 · Skills & aspirations

Made by choices, not destiny

Humans possess no universal innate magical gift or defining supernatural ability. Their strengths emerge through individual talent, training, opportunity, cooperation and determination.

A human might become a formidable wizard, a skilled craftsperson, a wandering adventurer, a quiet archivist, or someone who follows no extraordinary calling at all.

Their relatively short lives, compared with some long-lived peoples, can influence how they approach ambition, legacy and change—but never determine what an individual must value. Ageing is generally natural and gradual, without supernatural longevity as a defining trait of the species.

04 · Life at the Library

Visitors from every corner of the realms

The Grand Library welcomes humans as visitors, researchers, apprentices, diplomats, travellers and staff members, bringing countless languages, questions and perspectives.

Some seek forgotten histories; others pursue magical studies, practical knowledge, artistic inspiration, or simply a quiet place to read. Their reasons for coming are as varied as humanity itself.

The Library records individuals and communities on their own terms. No single human tradition, ambition or belief is treated as universal.
05 · Storytelling & roleplay

Adventure takes many paths

These archetypes are prompts for stories and roleplay, not fixed human types or required occupations. A person may fit several, or none at all.

The Wanderer

Explorers and travellers crossing borders in search of fortune, answers, or a place to belong.

The Scholar

Researchers, wizards, historians and students pursuing knowledge—and debating how it should be used.

The Opportunist

Merchants, bards, diplomats and negotiators who find possibilities in changing circumstances.

The Maker

Artisans, inventors, builders and creators who turn ideas into tools, objects and new ways of living.
