The Mycelials

The Mycelium-Born

Sentient fungal beings shaped by spores, living networks and countless individual histories. No two lives need take the same form.

01 · General classification

A thousand forms, a thousand lives.

Mycelials are sentient fungal beings found throughout the realms. Their appearances vary widely, from familiar mushroom-like forms to unusual, asymmetrical bodies covered in delicate gills, branching patterns or smaller fungal growths.

02 · Emergence & origins

Where a new consciousness begins

Mycelials emerge in two known ways: from spores that develop into new individuals, or from ancient mycelial networks that give rise to a new consciousness. The precise conditions behind these births remain incompletely understood.

Neither origin determines who a Mycelial will become. Their interests, temperaments, relationships and sense of identity develop through their own experiences.

03 · Form & physiology

Fungal bodies, individual differences

Mycelial bodies are formed from fungal tissue and mycelial structures. Some have recognisable caps and gills; others grow branching forms, layered surfaces or smaller fungi across their bodies. Their shapes do not follow a single standard.

Some release spores in response to emotions or meaningful experiences, while others display little visible spore activity. Such responses vary between individuals and should not be taken as a universal sign of any particular feeling.

Their lifespans and physical development also vary. The Library continues to document these differences without assuming that one individual's biology represents the whole people.

04 · Research & uncertainty

Memory and the Ancient Networks

Some ancient mycelial networks appear to preserve fragments of the past: impressions of places, sensations and events experienced long ago. Mycelials born from these networks may occasionally perceive traces of such memories, though they do not inherit complete recollections or the identities of those who came before them.

The nature of this phenomenon remains a subject of study and debate. Researchers have yet to establish whether these traces are stored memories, sensory echoes or something not yet described.

Research status: The ancient networks remain incompletely understood. Reports are recorded as observations, not treated as settled explanations.
05 · Communities & belonging

Life among the realms

Mycelials have no single culture or prescribed way of life. They can be found in forests, underground settlements, cities, gardens and libraries, pursuing countless professions and interests.

A shared fungal nature does not mean a shared personality, tradition or purpose. Their communities and customs vary, and individuals shape their own relationships with the wider world.

At the Grand Library, Mycelials are recognised as individuals with their own histories, identities and rights—not as specimens to be studied without consent.
