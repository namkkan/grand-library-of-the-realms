The Grand Atrium · The Heart of the Building

The Grand Central Hall

A monumental, symmetrical hall where the Library opens around its visitors: a place to pause, look upward, gather one's bearings and choose the next path.

The Grand Library / The Grand Atrium / The Grand Central Hall

The Central Hall is a public crossroads, not a reading room or a department. Its purpose is to welcome people into the scale of the Library and distribute movement towards the surrounding galleries, public services and connected districts.

01 · Architecture

A Palace at the Centre

Formal symmetry gives visitors a sense of order even when the institution beyond seems almost endless.

Explore details
The central volume

Monumental Proportions

The hall rises through several storeys, with a high vaulted ceiling and upper galleries overlooking the public floor. The ground-floor plan is the first stage; the vertical spaces can be developed later.

  • Strong central axis from the southern vestibule
  • Balanced west and east gallery entrances
  • Stone, dark timber and restrained gold detailing
  • Clear sightlines across the public floor
A visual landmark

The Compass Medallion

A large compass-like floor medallion marks the centre of the hall. It is a decorative landmark and meeting reference, not a magical device that decides where people may go.

  • Visible from the main entrance
  • A natural meeting point for groups
  • Directional motifs echo the Library's wayfinding
  • Kept clear for circulation and emergency access
02 · Movement & flow

Routes Through the Hall

The hall should feel grand without becoming confusing or obstructed.

Explore details
ARRIVAL

Southern Vestibule

Visitors enter from the south. Hyssop's reception remains to one side, before the hall, for welcome and first questions.

ORIENTATION

The Central Floor

The compass medallion and visible gallery entrances help visitors identify the main directions and pause without blocking the route.

DISTRIBUTION

West & East Galleries

Side passages lead towards the surrounding wings and onward connections. The west gallery already leads towards Natural Sciences.

Circulation rule: the main axis from the entrance through the hall stays open. Benches, signs, tour groups and temporary displays belong at the edges or in designated alcoves, never across the central route.
03 · Looking upward

Upper Galleries & Staircases

The vertical architecture makes the Central Hall feel like the heart of an interior city, while the initial plan remains focused on the ground floor.

Explore details
Later architectural layer

Grand Staircases

Two balanced staircases may rise along the sides of the hall to upper public galleries. Their landings can become visual overlooks and orientation points.

  • Symmetrical placement
  • Clear connection to marked public routes
  • Accessible alternatives signposted nearby
  • Restricted stairways visibly distinguished
Upper-level circulation

Balconies & Galleries

Upper galleries overlook the central floor and connect selected public routes. They are circulation spaces rather than shelf-lined reading galleries for now.

  • Balustrades and repeated arches
  • Views back towards the compass medallion
  • Directional signs at junctions
  • Future connections defined only when needed
Planning note: these upper levels are a future design layer, not a claim that their complete floor plans already exist.
04 · The feeling of the place

Sound, Light & Quiet Corners

The Central Hall is active and public, but it should not feel chaotic.

Explore details
Atmosphere

Light from Above

High windows, clerestories or a glazed roof can bring daylight into the centre. At night, warm lamps and restrained magical lights preserve the deep blue, stone and gold palette.

Pause without blocking

Edge Alcoves

Small seating niches along the perimeter give visitors somewhere to wait, check a map or regroup without turning the hall into a reading room.
