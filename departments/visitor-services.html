The Grand Atrium · Public Assistance

Visitor Services

Every journey through the Library begins with a question. Our staff help visitors find their way, understand how the institution works, and discover the people and collections best suited to their needs.

The Grand Library / The Grand Atrium / Visitor Services

Visitor Services is the Atrium's general assistance point—not a department of its own and not a substitute for specialist librarians. It connects visitors with the right information, route, service or member of staff.

01 · Ask and discover

Information & Enquiries

For first-time visitors, experienced researchers, curious travellers and anyone unsure where to begin.

General guidance

The Information Desk

Staff answer everyday questions and help visitors understand the Library's public spaces, facilities and procedures.

  • Directions and room locations
  • Opening arrangements and visitor guidance
  • Help with catalogues and directory systems
  • Referrals to specialist departments
Research support

Finding the Right Collection

Visitors can explain what they hope to find—even if they do not know the proper name for it—and receive guidance towards relevant catalogues or subject specialists.

  • Identifying likely collections
  • Finding a librarian or curator with relevant expertise
  • Understanding consultation requirements
  • Planning a route between connected districts
02 · A library for every visitor

Accessibility & Individual Assistance

Visitors should be able to ask for practical support without having to explain more than is necessary.

Routes and facilities

Finding a Suitable Route

Staff can help identify lifts, ramps, seating, quieter spaces and routes that avoid stairs or difficult passages where those options are available.

  • Step-free route information
  • Directions to accessible facilities
  • Assistance locating quieter areas
  • Alternative directions when a passage is closed
Communication

Clear Information

Where possible, information can be provided in writing, explained verbally or supported by the Living Directory. Staff assistance remains available if enchantments or devices fail.

  • Written directions and printed maps
  • Help interpreting signs and instructions
  • Communication support where available
  • A non-magical alternative to enchanted guidance
A guiding principle: magical assistance is optional. No visitor should have to rely on an enchantment to understand essential directions, request help or reach an available public service.

Visitor Guidance

First visit

Starting Your Visit

Visitors can learn where to go first, which areas are open to the public, and whether a particular room requires registration or an appointment.

Using the Library

Staff explain local rules in plain language and direct visitors to the relevant room guidelines before they enter a specialist or protected area.

04 · Take part

Events & Public Programme

What's happening

Activities & Events

When plans change

Practical Help

If an event moves, a route closes or a visitor becomes separated from their group, staff help them find the latest available information and the appropriate next step.

  • Updated directions
  • Help locating a meeting point
  • Lost-property enquiries
  • Contacting the relevant service team
05 · A simple journey

How Visitor Services Works

Not every question needs the same kind of answer. The service is designed to guide visitors onward, rather than keep them at the desk.

STEP I

Tell us what you need

Ask a question, describe what you are looking for, or explain what is making your visit difficult.

STEP II

Find the right help

Staff provide an answer, a clear route, a directory entry or a referral to someone with specialist knowledge.

STEP III

Continue your visit

Leave with the information you need, and return to the desk if your plans change or another question arises.

About the Living Directory: the Library's enchanted guidance network can help with routes and selected signs, but it cannot grant access permissions, read minds or guarantee that every passage is safe or open. Printed maps and staff guidance remain valid alternatives.
