02 · Find your way

Orientation & the Living Directory

The Library is too vast to navigate through memory alone. Its directory combines physical signs, catalogues, maps and a carefully limited network of enchantments.

EXPLORE THE GRAND DIRECTORY ↗
INFORMATION

The Directory Chamber

A public information point for finding collections, departments, staff offices, facilities and routes.

  • Printed and digital directories
  • Catalogue access terminals
  • Route planning between buildings
  • Information about access requirements
FANTASTICAL SYSTEM

The Living Directory

A magical guidance network that can illuminate routes, translate selected signs and adapt directions to a visitor's physical needs when reliable information is available.

  • Multilingual signs and spoken guidance
  • Routes that avoid stairs or unsuitable passages
  • Warnings when a route is closed or restricted
  • Non-magical maps and staff assistance as alternatives

Limits of the enchantment

The Living Directory does not read minds, grant permissions or reveal restricted collections. Ancient wards, damaged routes and unstable magical conditions may interrupt it. In protected historical areas, the system can be deliberately limited to avoid harming the building or its collections.

05 · Permissions

Access & Security

Access rules protect people, collections and research without turning the Library into a fortress. Restrictions must have a clear purpose and apply fairly to all visitors.

Open

Public Areas

The Atrium, public plazas, general directories, designated reading rooms and open cultural activities.

Registered

Supervised Access

Certain consultation rooms, specialist services and materials that require registration, an appointment or staff supervision.

Restricted

Protected Areas

Heritage storage, private archives, delicate laboratories and dangerous or protected collections requiring specific permission.

The principle of equal access: access decisions depend on the needs of a service, the condition of an item and justified safety requirements — never on a visitor's species, wealth, ancestry or social prestige. Necessary adaptations for different bodies, senses and forms of communication are part of the Library's ordinary design.
06 · Beyond the Atrium

Galleries, Plazas & Connections

The Atrium is the starting point of a much larger living city: a network of buildings accumulated over centuries, connected by covered passages, bridges, stairs and public squares.

THE PLAZAS

Public Gathering Spaces

Open areas for meeting, exhibitions, announcements, public ceremonies and activities that bring different communities together.

THE GALLERIES

Covered Passages

Corridors, stairways, bridges and elevated walkways linking buildings from different periods and architectural traditions.

THE HISTORICAL COMPLEXES

Buildings Across the Ages

Former halls, converted residences, monumental archives and newer structures incorporated into the growing institution.

A city within a library

A visitor may cross a medieval stone gallery, pass beneath a Victorian iron bridge and emerge into a modern public square without ever leaving the Library. Some connections are obvious; others have been forgotten, sealed, rebuilt or altered by magic. The public directory records safe routes, while the existence of every hidden passage is not necessarily disclosed.
