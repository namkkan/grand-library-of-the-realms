The Grand Directory

A guide to a library too vast for any single map. Find a district, plan a route, locate a collection, or ask the Living Directory to illuminate the way.

The Grand Library / The Grand Atrium / The Grand Directory

The Grand Directory is the Library's navigation system. It brings together physical maps, catalogues, district guides and magical wayfinding. It helps visitors decide where to go; Visitor Services helps when they need a person to guide them.

02 · Read the building

Maps & Physical Signage

Maps remain available even when magical systems are unavailable, unreliable or deliberately limited.

At a glance

Public Maps

Printed plans and fixed signs show public routes, major rooms, exits and accessible alternatives where they are available.

  • Ground-floor plans at major entrances
  • District and building names
  • Public facilities and meeting points
  • Warnings for closed or restricted passages
Beyond the Atrium

District Guides

Each documented district can maintain its own guide with local room names, collection locations, access requirements and onward connections.

  • Local room and collection indexes
  • Connections to neighbouring districts
  • Stairs, lifts and alternative routes
  • Notes on appointments or permissions
03 · Find knowledge

Catalogues & Collection Indexes

Finding a room and finding a particular work are different tasks. The Directory points visitors towards the right catalogue for each.

Where is it?

Location Index

Use room names, district names and collection identifiers to discover where an item or service is held, when that information is available.

What is it?

Subject & Collection Catalogues

Specialist catalogues describe materials by subject, creator, culture, language, format or other relevant characteristics. Some collections require additional guidance before consultation.

04 · The enchanted guide

The Living Directory

An enchanted wayfinding network woven into selected signs, directory panels and public routes throughout the Library.

What it can do

Guidance in Motion

  • Illuminate a known route between documented destinations
  • Offer selected translations and spoken directions
  • Suggest alternatives when stairs or passages are unsuitable
  • Warn visitors when a route is known to be closed
What it cannot do

Limits & Safeguards

  • It cannot grant permission to enter restricted areas
  • It cannot guarantee every corridor is stable or safe
  • It may fail around ancient wards or damaged enchantments
  • It does not replace maps, staff or local instructions
Rule of the Directory: a route being visible does not mean it is permitted. Access notices and the instructions of authorised staff always take precedence over magical guidance.
05 · Plan your route

From Question to Destination

For most visitors, finding their way through the Library follows three simple steps.

STEP I

Find the district

Use the district directory or ask at the Atrium to identify the right part of the Library.

STEP II

Check the route

Consult the building map, local signage and any access or appointment requirements.

STEP III

Ask if uncertain

Visitor Services can clarify directions and refer you to staff with specialist knowledge.
