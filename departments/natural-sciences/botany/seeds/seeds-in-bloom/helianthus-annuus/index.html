SUNFLOWER

Helianthus annuus L.

A study of the sunflower's familiar achene: its protective hull, oil-rich reserves, and the first stages of a new plant's growth.

PROFESSOR ELOWEN THORNE · DEPARTMENT OF BOTANY
01 · Botanical identification

Identity of the Specimen

  • Common name: Sunflower
  • Scientific name: Helianthus annuus L.
  • Family: Asteraceae
  • Plant type: Annual flowering plant
  • Seed category: Oilseed; edible seed
  • Origin: North America; later domesticated and cultivated worldwide.

The sunflower is recognised by its large flower head, which contains many individual florets. Each fertile floret may develop into a dry fruit commonly called a sunflower seed.

02 · Seed morphology

The Achene and Its Hull

The familiar sunflower “seed” is botanically a fruit called an achene. It is dry, indehiscent and usually elongated or oval, with a slightly flattened profile and a firm outer hull.

External appearance

The hull may be black, grey, white, cream or brown, often with contrasting stripes. Its surface is generally firm and smooth, although appearance varies by cultivar.

Internal structure

Inside the hull is the true seed, containing a well-developed embryo with two fleshy cotyledons. These store oils and other reserves used during early growth.

04 · Germination and early development

From Fruit to Seedling

Stage I — Imbibition

The fruit absorbs water, initiating metabolic activity inside the seed. The embryo resumes growth.

Stage II — Radicle emergence

The embryonic root breaks through the covering structures and begins anchoring the developing plant.

Stage III — Epigeal emergence

The hypocotyl elongates and lifts the cotyledons above the soil. They help nourish the seedling and can begin photosynthesis.

Germination speed depends on seed viability, temperature, moisture and growing conditions. Record observations from the actual specimen rather than treating one timetable as universal.
05 · Ecological and practical significance

A Plant of Many Uses

Sunflower seeds are rich in oil and are important in human food production, cooking-oil manufacture and animal feed. They also provide food for birds and other wildlife. Cultivation has produced varieties with different hull colours, sizes and oil contents.

Taxonomic note: In everyday language these are called sunflower seeds. Technically, the familiar hard outer “shell” belongs to the fruit wall, while the true seed lies inside it.

06 · Notes for the botanical illustrator

Suggested Observations

  • Compare achene length, width, profile and hull markings across different cultivars.
  • Show the fruit wall separately from the true seed in a labelled section.
  • Record the two cotyledons and the position of the embryonic root.
  • Sketch the transition from germinating achene to emerging seedling.
A familiar object often hides a more intricate structure. The sunflower's “seed” is a fruit carrying a seed within it.
