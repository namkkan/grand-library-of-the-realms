Chapter I · The Living Plant

Botanical Description

Cosmos bipinnatus is a fast-growing annual native to Mexico. Depending on variety and conditions, it may reach approximately 60 cm to over 2 m. Its slender, branching stems carry fine, feathery leaves divided into thread-like segments. Flower heads resemble open daisies, with white, pink, magenta or violet outer florets surrounding a central disc of tiny yellow florets.

It flowers through warmer months and may continue into autumn until cold weather or frost. Cosmos thrives in full sun and generally prefers moderately fertile, well-drained soil. Excessively rich soil can encourage foliage at the expense of flowers.

Chapter II · Form and Identification

Seed Morphology

The structures commonly called seeds are technically achenes: dry, one-seeded fruits that do not split open at maturity.

  • Shape: long, narrow and slightly angular, like a needle or slender splinter.
  • Colour: usually dark brown to nearly black at maturity.
  • Surface: smooth or faintly textured, sometimes with longitudinal ridges.
  • Tip: often narrowed into a short beak; some fruits have two or three bristle-like awns.
  • Length: often approximately 7–15 mm, depending on the specimen.
Chapter III · The Cycle of Formation

From Flower to Seed

Each flower head contains many florets. After pollination and fertilisation, fertile florets may form narrow fruits. The petals fade, the centre dries and the fruits darken. Collect only when the fruits themselves are mature and dry; if left on the plant, they may fall and produce seedlings.

Chapter IV · Renewal

Germination and Propagation

  • Sow outdoors after danger of frost has passed, or start in containers.
  • Cover lightly; avoid burying the narrow fruits too deeply.
  • Provide plenty of direct sunlight.
  • Keep soil lightly moist but not waterlogged.
  • Germination often takes 7–21 days; flowering may begin around 2–3 months after sowing.

Cosmos can self-seed readily. Remove spent heads for continued flowering, but leave selected heads to mature if collecting seed.

Chapter VI · Preservation

Seed Collection and Storage

  1. Select healthy plants with desired characteristics.
  2. Leave selected flower heads until fruits are mature and dry.
  3. Collect before fruits scatter naturally.
  4. Finish drying in a dry, well-ventilated place if needed.
  5. Store in a labelled paper envelope in a cool, dry location.

Cross-pollination between nearby varieties can produce offspring with different flower colours or forms, so saved seed may not reproduce a named cultivar exactly.

Chapter VII · Field Notebook

Field Notes

Use these observations to compare specimens across seasons and growing conditions.

Foliage: record the fine leaf divisions, plant height and branching.

Flower head: record outer-floret colour, central disc, flowering dates and visiting insects.

Fruits: observe colour, ridges, tapered tips and any bristles.

Ecology: note pollinators and other wildlife visiting the plant.
