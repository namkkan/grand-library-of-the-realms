Professor Elowen Thorne

Record 001 · Introduction

Overview

Elowen Thorne teaches botany at the Grand Library. Most of her time is spent with plants, field notes and books that are usually open on three different tables at once. She is known for her work on plant classification, especially species that are difficult to identify or don't quite fit the existing records.

She can be hard to talk to if you don't know her. Elowen is direct, gets impatient when people make claims without evidence, and sometimes forgets that not everyone has spent the last six hours thinking about the same plant. She isn't trying to be unkind. She just doesn't always notice how she comes across.

“Preserving a plant is not the same as preserving the place it needs to live.”

Appearance

She's an older woman with a weathered face, pale blue eyes and round spectacles. Her hair is white or silver and usually tied back in a way that suggests she did it quickly and then forgot about it. She wears practical clothes with far too many pockets: linen shirts, waistcoats, sturdy boots and things that have clearly been repaired rather than replaced.

Her fingers are often stained with ink or soil. There are usually pencils, labels, seed envelopes or a notebook somewhere on her person. She rarely looks dressed for a formal lecture, even when she is giving one.

Things she carries

  • A battered notebook full of observations and small drawings.
  • Pencils, specimen labels and a magnifying glass.
  • Seed envelopes and little containers she means to put away later.
  • Some kind of magical botanical tool — I haven't decided what it is yet.

Faculty & Fieldwork

What She Does

Elowen studies plants, writes descriptions and tries to make sense of the ones that other people have labelled incorrectly or given up on. She spends a lot of time outdoors collecting observations, but she is just as happy comparing old records in the Library. Her book, The Living Index, is the work she's best known for.

Classification matters to her because names and records affect what happens to a species. If a rare plant is mistaken for a common one, it might be collected until there's nothing left. If nobody records where it grows, it's much harder to protect that place.

She cares about conservation, although she can be stubborn about how things should be done. She keeps seeds and specimens when it makes sense, but she knows that moving a plant into a greenhouse doesn't save the habitat it came from.

Background

A Life Outdoors

As a child, Elowen was happier wandering around outside than sitting in a crowded room. She would return to the same patch of woodland to see what had changed, draw plants in her notebooks and watch insects for far longer than anyone expected her to.

She wasn't necessarily a lonely child. She just liked having something to look at and think about, without having to talk all the time. Plants were full of things she didn't understand yet, and she wanted to work them out. The drawings turned into notes, the notes turned into proper records, and eventually this became her work.

As an adult, she arranged her life around research. Invitations and dinners gradually became things she could usually do without. Her home filled up with books, labelled samples, pots on the windowsill and tools left out to dry. It's comfortable to her, even if someone else might call it a mess.

Personality

What She's Like

Elowen is curious, capable and very independent. She can spend an entire afternoon looking at one specimen and not realise how late it has become. She likes being able to explain how something works, but learning more about it doesn't make it less interesting to her.

She prefers a small amount of company to a busy social life. She can enjoy a good conversation and care about people without wanting them around all the time. She has made a life she likes, and she doesn't secretly need someone to come along and fix it for her.

Her biggest problem is that she expects other people to be as precise and focused as she is. She can be patient with a difficult plant for hours, then lose patience with a person in a few minutes. She knows this about herself, though knowing it and changing it are two different things.

Habits & Contradictions

The Difficult Bits

She wants things done properly.

If someone changes a specimen label without explaining why, she'll go back through the notes to find out what happened. She checks other people's work even when they have already checked it themselves. She believes records should be corrected when new evidence appears, but has a hard time trusting someone else's process.

She's honest, sometimes too honest.

If a conclusion doesn't make sense, she says so. She might point out a mistake in front of a whole room and only realise later that the person was embarrassed. She doesn't set out to humiliate people, but that doesn't mean it never happens.

She forgets to stop.

If a plant is in danger, she keeps working. Meals get missed, tea goes cold and she tells herself she'll sleep after one more thing. There is always one more thing.

She doesn't like being corrected.

She knows she can be wrong, and she will change her mind when the evidence is there. Getting to that point is the hard part, especially if the person who spotted the mistake is younger or less experienced.

She finds feelings harder than facts.

If someone she cares about is having a bad time, she'll try to solve the problem. She might bring food, find a book, repair something or quietly make sure they get home safely. Asking them to talk about it is much less natural to her.

Personal Notes

How She Shows She Cares

Elowen isn't very good at saying affectionate things out loud. She remembers small details, though, and often does something useful with them. If someone mentions a plant they loved as a child, she might save seeds for them and leave the packet on their desk without making a big deal out of it.

She can go a long time without writing to someone. If she misses them, she is more likely to invent a reason to ask them a research question than to say she wants to see them. Apologising is difficult too. She can admit a mistake in her work, but admitting she hurt someone's feelings is another matter.

Once, a young researcher came to her worried about a population of magical flowers that seemed to be disappearing. Elowen found a mistake in the measurements and told her so rather bluntly. Later that evening, she checked the records again and realised that the warning itself had been right.

By morning, Elowen had organised an expedition. She didn't manage a graceful apology. She simply told the researcher:

“Your measurements were imprecise. Your concern was justified. We leave at dawn.”

That is probably closer to an apology from Elowen than most people will get. She may not always know what to say, but she will show up when it matters.

Current Interests

Magical Botany

Elowen wants to know what magic actually does to plants. If a flower glows, is it a natural trait, a magical effect, or a bit of both? If a plant grows in an enchanted place for generations, does it become a different species? And how can you tell whether a plant is thinking, or simply reacting in a way that looks like thought?

She doesn't accept “it's magic” as a complete explanation. She wants observations, comparisons and records that someone else can check. She is also quite willing to admit that the answer might be stranger than she expected, once she has enough evidence.

Still to decide

Her close relationships, more details of her past, and the exact nature of her magical research tool are still open. This record can grow as those parts of her story take shape.
