Record 002 · Introduction

Overview

Hyssop is a curious, mushroom-like being who welcomes newcomers to the Grand Library. He knows its public routes, remembers obscure details, and can turn even the simplest question into a long and enthusiastic conversation.

He wants visitors to feel that they belong here. His explanations sometimes wander, but his enthusiasm is genuine, and he rarely lets a newcomer leave without feeling a little less lost.

“A good guide doesn't just show you where to go. They make you want to see what's there.”

Appearance

Hyssop is short and round, with an unmistakably asymmetrical silhouette. His irregular cap tilts to one side, framing large, round, expressive eyes. Delicate gills sit beneath the cap, while small mushrooms and organic growths emerge unevenly across his body. He has no hair, and his physical form remains stable throughout his long life.

He dresses in a somewhat dishevelled, Victorian-inspired outfit: usually a burgundy coat, a cream waistcoat, antique brass buttons and a monocle suspended from a fine chain. His many pockets are full of notes, labels and miscellaneous objects he has collected along the way.

Signature Detail

When Hyssop becomes absorbed in a book or feels he has genuinely helped someone, he releases a tiny cloud of pale, almost golden spores. It is involuntary—the fungal equivalent of blushing—and he rarely notices it himself.

Personality

A Curious Nature

Hyssop remembers obscure facts and gets distracted by fascinating details. A simple request for directions can become an impromptu lecture about a forgotten reading room, an unusual staircase or a visitor from centuries ago.

He is most animated when a book catches his attention or when a newcomer leaves feeling less lost. Those are the moments when the little clouds of spores tend to appear.

Hyssop is a cheerful, slightly chaotic clerk with the instincts of a practiced host. He may begin a sentence while searching through three piles of notes, remember a fascinating detail halfway through an answer, and finish with a joke that makes a nervous visitor relax. His desk can look untidy to others, but he usually knows where the important things are.

His humour is welcoming rather than sharp. He is happy to tease a familiar colleague or make a light-hearted remark about the Library's confusing corridors, but he notices when someone feels overwhelmed. With a first-time visitor, he slows down, listens carefully and breaks directions into manageable steps instead of making them feel foolish for asking.

Hyssop knows many of the Library's regular visitors and staff by sight, name or some small detail from a previous conversation. He remembers who prefers a printed map, who needs a quieter route, and who always asks for tea. He does not know everything, however, and is comfortable finding the correct person when a question falls outside his role.

Background

Discovery in the Library

Hyssop was discovered in a damp basement near the Department of Botany. He had awakened alone and was already moving books around when the first staff members found him. No one could quite work out what he was doing, but he seemed to know exactly where each volume belonged—or at least had a very enthusiastic explanation for every change.

Before long, he began interrupting formal tours to add forgotten details and correct historical inaccuracies. Visitors started asking for him by name. The Library eventually recognised what they already knew: nobody could make a newcomer feel more welcome.

Library Role

Hyssop's Responsibilities

Hyssop works in Welcome & Arrival, helping visitors orient themselves, understand the public areas and find the right entrance, service or route. He is especially good with first-time visitors, who can find the Library's scale overwhelming.

For Hyssop, guiding is more than a job. It is a chance to share the wonder of the Library with someone who has never experienced it before. He may take a little longer than expected, but he wants every visitor to leave with a sense that they belong here.

The Living Directory

Hyssop helps visitors use the Library's Living Directory: he interprets its directions, explains unfamiliar district names, checks a route against the physical map and offers a non-magical alternative when the enchantment is unavailable or unsuitable. He knows the public routes well, but he does not grant access to restricted rooms or promise that every old passage is safe.

A Small Welcome

His welcome is informal and personal. He greets visitors, offers a little tea when appropriate, and gives people a moment to settle before sending them on their way. He is especially attentive to newcomers who seem intimidated by the scale of the Library. The tea is an invitation, never an obligation.

The Key Cabinet

A small cabinet near his station holds keys used for authorised everyday access. Hyssop keeps the cabinet orderly enough to work, checks which key is needed and follows the Library's permission rules. He is friendly, but he does not lend keys simply because someone asks nicely; the relevant authorisation must come first.

Lost property, messages and correspondence are handled by other members of the Library staff. Hyssop may point a visitor towards the appropriate colleague, but those services are not part of his own post.

His Place at the Entrance

The Reception Nook

Hyssop works at a side-positioned reception desk in the western side of the Southern Vestibule, just before visitors pass into the Grand Central Hall. It is deliberately off the main line of traffic: easy to find, but not blocking the entrance or turning the vestibule into a queue of desks.

The desk is an old oak piece with a gently worn edge, shallow drawers, brass fittings and a few small repairs made over its long working life. Its shape is formal enough to belong in a palace-like library, while the objects gathered on it make it feel unmistakably lived-in. Subtle mycelial growths and tiny organic details have gradually settled into the wood around the edges, as though the desk and its keeper have quietly grown accustomed to one another. These details are part of the setting, not a replacement for ordinary furniture or accessible equipment.

Hyssop's personal nook includes a small brass Bell of Arrival, a Visitor's Ledger for notable visits and enquiries that need a record, and an old printed visitor map for anyone who would rather follow a page than a magical guide. A modest tea set sits within easy reach. His nameplate is visible from the entrance, and the Key Cabinet is kept close enough for him to supervise it without making it the centrepiece of the welcome.

The nook is busy without being grand: notes tucked beneath a paperweight, a few labelled slips, a cup that is nearly always nearby, and a chair or small resting spot for moments between arrivals. Hyssop's belongings have accumulated gradually. There is a place for the things he uses every day, even if a visitor might need to ask him which pile that is.

The bell marks a visitor's arrival; it does not summon a magical response or reveal anything about the person who rings it. The ledger is used with discretion, and the old map remains available if the Living Directory cannot help. Hyssop's tea is a small gesture of hospitality rather than a formal service, and the key cabinet follows the same access rules as the rest of the Library.

A Welcoming First Point of Contact

Hyssop's nook is designed around a simple idea: a visitor should be able to pause, ask a question and receive help without feeling that they have interrupted something important. The grand architecture may be imposing; the reception itself should feel approachable.

Hyssop belongs to the Mycelials, also called the Mycelium-born. Their species archive records varied fungal forms, spore-born lives and the ancient networks whose workings remain uncertain. Hyssop's stable shape, habits and involuntary golden spores belong to his own record; they do not describe every Mycelial.
